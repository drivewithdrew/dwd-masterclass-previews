Rule 126 · Stopping distance

23 m total · 9 m thinking + 14 m braking

Highway Code typical dry-road figure at 30 mph. Actual distances vary with reaction time, tyres, weather and road surface.

Drew's three golden scanning questions

  1. What can I see? Active hazards, road markings and traffic ahead.
  2. What CAN'T I see? Blind bends, crests and hidden gaps between parked vans.
  3. Can I stop comfortably within the distance I can see to be clear? If not, ease off immediately.

Drew's coaching focus

A speed limit is a legal ceiling, not a target. Safe does not mean crawling along at 20 mph on a clear open road—that may be marked as a lack of progress. Equally, doing 30 mph through narrow residential pinch points is dangerous. Read the road, ease off the accelerator early to let the engine do the work, and remember: never put the car where your eyes haven't just been.

DVSA examiner fault benchmarks ▾

Illustrative severity examples, not automatic DVSA classifications. Examiners assess actual risk, context and consequences.

🟡 Driving fault: Slightly too slow on an open clear road without cause, or delayed acceleration after a hazard clears, with limited effect.

🔴 Serious fault: Speed inappropriate for conditions, exceeding a limit in circumstances presenting significant potential danger, or sustained undue hesitation creating significant risk.

⛔ Dangerous fault: Excessive speed creates actual danger, requiring emergency action by others or examiner intervention.

Highway Code essentials

Rule 125: Speed limits are maximums, not necessarily safe speeds. Rule 126: Leave enough space to stop safely; typical stopping distances on dry roads: 20 mph = 12 m, 30 mph = 23 m, 40 mph = 36 m, 50 mph = 53 m, 60 mph = 73 m, 70 mph = 96 m. Rule 146: Adapt driving to road and traffic conditions; be prepared for unexpected hazards.

Remember

Don't crawl without reason. Don't chase the limit into danger. Ease off early, scan ahead, and regain safe progress when the hazard has cleared.
